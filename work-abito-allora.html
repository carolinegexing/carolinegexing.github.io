Abito Allora

How I help a luxury vintage store in Zurich earn trust online, when every piece is one of a kind.

Role
Store & Digital Marketing Manager
Scope
Website, campaigns, content, analytics
Stack
Squarespace, Meta Business Suite, GA4
Team
Solo, reporting to the founder

The challenge and my approach

Abito Allora is a luxury vintage store in Zurich, and every piece we sell is one of a kind.

That means the standard e-commerce playbook does not apply. A sold bag cannot be restocked, and nobody spends four figures on a second-hand Hermès without trusting it is authentic.

  • Every piece is unique, so stock cannot be repeated
  • Customers have doubts about authenticity, condition and how we set prices
  • The online shop and the store felt like two separate businesses
  • No paid budget, and a team of one

Rebuilt the online shop around trust

I restructured navigation and product discovery for mobile first, and added multilingual support and customer logins. I also wrote the two pages that did most of the work, our About page and a clear explanation of how consignment works, including how we set prices. Most recently we launched Entrupy, a globally recognised authentication service that helps customers verify items they bought elsewhere.

  • Squarespace
  • Information architecture
  • SEO
  • Copy

Ran a 24-day advent calendar

For 24 days we published new content on Stories, Reels and Facebook, each post tied to a real item in the shop. Stories generated 73.8% of all engagement, so we concentrated the daily posts there. Filming in-store supplied the content, and the content brought people back into the store.

  • Content
  • Instagram
  • Campaign planning

Measured each channel for its real role

The data showed the newsletter was not converting. Instead, it sent readers to Instagram. The website was not a browsing destination either, since customers used it to check our credibility before buying. I stopped judging the newsletter on sales and the website on browsing, and measured each against the role it actually played.

  • GA4
  • Meta Business Suite
  • Attribution

Connected the channels

Email introduced each campaign, the website provided the proof, social media carried the daily conversation, and the store sent people back to our online channels. The goal was repeated, consistent exposure across touchpoints.

  • Customer journey
  • CRM
  • In-store

What moved

Measured across the campaign period, with no paid media budget.

+29%new users to the website, 70% on mobile

What the data showed

  • Email was a bridge to social media, not the place where people bought
  • Customers used the website to verify us, not to browse
  • Mobile dominated every channel
  • Stories drove 73.8% of engagement

What I would do next

  • Improve tracking and attribution across channels
  • Expand SEO beyond our brand keywords
  • Build content formats we can repeat, instead of one-off campaigns
  • Add reviews and talk more about how we authenticate

For a luxury second-hand business, trust has to be earned first.
